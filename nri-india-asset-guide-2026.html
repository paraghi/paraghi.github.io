⚡ Critical Rule: Section 49(1) — Cost of Inherited Assets
When a capital asset is inherited, the heir's cost of acquisition is the cost at which the previous owner (last purchaser) acquired it — NOT market value at inheritance. The holding period also includes the previous owner's holding. For assets purchased before April 1, 2001, Fair Market Value as on that date may be substituted. This single provision can mean a ₹10–30 lakh difference in tax.

⚠ Finance Act 2020 — The 120-Day Rule
An Indian citizen or PIO who visits India for 120 or more days AND whose total Indian-sourced income exceeds ₹15 lakh is now deemed Resident (RNOR) even without being a tax resident in any other country. This plugged the 'stateless individual' loophole.
RNOR Status — The Re-Entry Buffer (Maximum 2 Years)

Qualifies as RNOR if either condition is met:

Condition A: Was Non-Resident in 9 out of the 10 preceding financial years
Condition B: Total stay in India in the 7 preceding financial years did not exceed 729 days
💡 RNOR Tax Strategy
The RNOR window is the ideal time to: (1) liquidate foreign mutual funds, bonds and property, (2) remit foreign income to India and park in NRE/RFC accounts, (3) delay starting India-sourced income streams. Foreign income during RNOR years is NOT taxable in India.

Inherited Property — Tax & Compliance

Section 49(1) is the most impactful provision in NRI taxation

Section 49(1) — The Master Rule
Cost of acquired asset = Last purchaser's cost (not market value at inheritance). Holding period includes previous owner's period. Pre-2001 assets: may use Fair Market Value as on April 1, 2001 (Registered Valuer certificate required).

05
Sameer Bose — The "Deemed Let-Out" Trap (Vacant Properties)
📍 Toronto, Canada | 2 inherited vacant flats in Kolkata
▼
Trap
Sameer declares both vacant flats as 'self-occupied'. From FY 2024-25, maximum TWO properties can be self-occupied. But Sameer has only 2 — so both are fine for FY 2024-25 onwards (limit extended from 1 to 2). Any THIRD property becomes deemed let-out even if genuinely vacant.
Section 23(1)(c) — Deemed Let-Out
For the property that is 'deemed let out', Annual Value = market rent, even if ₹0 rent was actually collected. Only relief: 30% standard deduction (Sec 24a) applies on this notional income. NRIs with multiple inherited properties must plan carefully.

🚨 TDS on Tenants Paying NRI Landlords — Most Overlooked Rule
Tenants must deduct TDS at 30% under Section 195 on ALL rent paid to NRI landlords — from the very first rupee. No monthly threshold (unlike ₹50,000/month limit for resident landlords). If tenant fails to deduct: tenant becomes 'assessee in default' and is personally liable for tax + interest.

Tax Planning Strategies for NRIs

Legal, commonly used strategies — sequencing matters

Strategy 1 — Account Selection (First-Order Decision)
Route all foreign income exclusively through NRE or FCNR(B) accounts. Interest is completely tax-free. The same ₹50 lakh earning interest in NRO = ₹15 lakh TDS per year. Single most impactful decision an NRI can make.

Strategy 3 — Lower Deduction Certificate (Section 197)

TDS on property sales is on the FULL sale price, not just the gain. For a ₹2.2 crore property with actual tax of ₹25L, TDS at 12.5% = ₹27.5L (over-deduction of ₹2.5L). LDC prevents this.

StepAction
1. FilingFile Form 13 with Jurisdictional Assessing Officer — at least 2-3 months before sale
2. DocumentsCapital gains computation, cost documents, PAN, estimated tax liability
3. CertificateAO issues LDC specifying lower/nil TDS rate for that specific transaction
4. ValidityOne financial year only — must renew annually
5. BenefitBuyer deducts TDS at LDC rate (can be as low as nil) instead of 12.5%/30%
Strategy 4 — Maximise the RNOR Window on Return
During RNOR Years (up to 2 years)
Foreign salary, business income and interest: NOT taxable in India. Ideal time to liquidate foreign MFs, bonds, property. Remit foreign income to India — park in NRE/RFC accounts before full Resident status.
Avoid During RNOR
Starting India-sourced income streams too early. Taking up Indian employment before RNOR window is used up. Keeping foreign assets that will be taxed once Resident status kicks in.

NRI Compliance Checklist

5-step action framework — from acquiring NRI status to annual compliance

Step 1 — On Acquiring NRI Status (Immediately)
  • Convert resident savings account to NRO account
  • Open NRE account for all future foreign remittances
  • Close or maintain PPF only until maturity — do NOT extend
  • Register for PIS (Portfolio Investment Scheme) before trading equities
  • Update address with all MF folios, DPs and insurance companies
  • Link PAN with Aadhaar if PAN was issued in India
Step 2 — When Inheriting Assets
  • Obtain legal heir certificate / succession certificate / probate as applicable
  • Register property mutation in name at local sub-registrar
  • Get shares / MF units transmitted via depository participant (DP)
  • Obtain Registered Valuer certificate for FMV as on April 1, 2001 (pre-2001 assets)
  • Do NOT treat inheritance value as cost — use previous owner's cost (Sec 49(1))
  • Check FEMA restrictions if agricultural land is inherited
Step 3 — Before Selling Any Asset
  • Apply for Lower Deduction Certificate (Form 13 / Sec 197) — at least 2-3 months before sale
  • Compute capital gains: correct cost (Sec 49(1)), correct holding period, correct indexation option
  • File Form 10F + TRC with payer for DTAA benefits before payment
  • Consider Sec 54 / 54EC / 54F reinvestment to reduce or eliminate LTCG
  • For joint ownership with resident: ensure TDS correctly bifurcated
Step 4 — For Repatriation of Funds
  • All sale proceeds / rental income must first go into NRO account
  • Form 15CA (self-declaration) + Form 15CB (CA certificate) mandatory for amounts > ₹5 lakh
  • Annual limit: USD 1 million per financial year from NRO
  • Agricultural / farmhouse land proceeds: CANNOT be repatriated — RBI approval required
  • Maintain records for 7 years for potential Income Tax scrutiny
